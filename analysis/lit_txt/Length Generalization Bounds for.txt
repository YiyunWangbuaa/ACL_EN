C-RASP
A programming language defined by Yang & Chiang 2024 based on
RASP invented by Weiss et al. 2021.
Dyck Language
Ca(i) :=# [j ≤ i] a(j) The number of a up to positioni (1)
Cb(i) :=# [j ≤ i] b(j) The number of b up to positioni (2)
V (i) :=Ca(i) < Cb(i) Violation: there are moreb than a (3)
CV (i) :=# [j ≤ i] V (j) The number of Violations (4)
M(i) :=CV (i) = 0 Matched i.e. zero Violations (5)
B(i) :=Ca(i) =Cb(i) Balanced i.e. same number ofa and b (6)
D(i) :=M(i) ∧ B(i) String is Matched and Balanced (7)
Yang et al. 2024 showed that transformers can simulate C-RASP
programs.

C-RASP: uncomputable bounds
For any Diophantine system of equations. . .
D := x 4 + y 4 + z 4 = w 4
There is a C-RASP program ϕD such that D has a solution . . .
x = 95800, y = 217519, z = 414560, w = 422481
whenever ϕD accepts some word. . .

We show that the undecidability of Diophantine equations implies
uncomputable length generalization bounds forC-RASP.

Summary
• We show C-RASP programs (and transformers) have uncomputable
length complexity via a reduction from Diophantine equations
• We show the positive fragment C-RASP + (and finite precision
transformers) have exponential length complexity
