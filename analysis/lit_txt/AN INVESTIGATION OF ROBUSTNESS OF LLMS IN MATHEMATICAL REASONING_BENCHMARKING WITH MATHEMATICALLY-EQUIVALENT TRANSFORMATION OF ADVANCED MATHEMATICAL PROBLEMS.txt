ABSTRACT
Frontier large language models (LLMs) now reach near-ceiling accuracy on stan-
dard mathematical-reasoning benchmarks and gold-medal-level performance at
the International Mathematical Olympiad. As these benchmarks saturate and their
items leak into training data, a high score no longer shows whether a model rea-
sons robustly, nor which component of its reasoning fails when it does not. To
evaluate reasoning in a way that remains informative for such models and makes
its failures diagnosable, we propose a novel evaluation methodology calledGAP
(Generalisation–and–Perturbation), where the key idea is to automatically gener-
ate many mathematically-equivalent variants of an existing mathematics problem
at scale, using two disjoint interpretable transformations: 1)surface renames,
which probe the binding between identifiers and latent variable roles, and 2)ker-
nel rewrites, which probe whether the high-level proof plan survives a change of
mathematical setting. Compared with existing benchmarks, a benchmark created
using GAP has two important benefits: 1) Since the new variants generated are
unlikely “seen" by an LLM, a GAP benchmark effectively addresses the data
leakage problem. 2) A GAP benchmark enables diagnosis of reasoning failures
based on the performance of an LLM over a spectrum of variants of mathematics
problems designed with different transformations, each corresponding to a mean-
ingful hypothesis about the cause of failure. We instantiate GAP on all of the 1,051
William Lowell Putnam Competition problems from 1938 to 2024, adding 5,255
unseen transformed instances to formPutnamGAP, a 6,306-item competition-level
mathematics corpus, resulting in the first public machine-readable dataset built
from thefullPutnam archive. Using PutnamGAP, we evaluated 18 commercial
and open-source models spanning different sizes and providers, and found that
accuracy drops across all models and variant families, with the largest degradations
under kernel rewrites. This gap does not close with model strength, suggesting
that the dominant remaining weakness of even the strongest models is carrying a
proof plan into a changed setting, not handling surface changes. Further diagnostic
analysis enables more detailed understanding of the causes of failure and generates
potentially useful insights for future research on improving the reasoning capacity
of LLMs.

1 INTRODUCTION
Reasoning models that scale inference-time computation (Snell et al., 2024; OpenAI, 2024; DeepSeek-
AI, 2025) have turned mathematics from a weakness of large language models into one of their
headline capabilities, from olympiad medals (Hubert et al., 2025; Google DeepMind, 2025) to near-
ceiling accuracy on long-standing benchmarks (Cobbe et al., 2021; Hendrycks et al., 2021). The
benchmarks built to measure this progress are being exhausted as quickly: static item sets saturate,
their contents leak into training corpora (Sainz et al., 2023; Balloccu et al., 2024), and even harder
successors (Glazer et al., 2024; Balunovi´c et al., 2025) record how often a model is right rather than
what it has learned.
A high score may reflect robust proof construction, memorized solutions, or successful exploitation
of surface cues; a low score under a modified problem shows that something broke, but not what
broke. This paper asks a more diagnostic question: when a model fails on a transformed mathematical
problem that it could solve in canonical form, which component of its reasoning failed?
Perturbation benchmarks partially address this problem by testing whether models remain accurate
after paraphrases, numerical changes, variable renames, or semantic modifications (Li et al., 2024;
Mirzadeh et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025; Gulati et al., 2025). These evaluations establish that
brittleness exists. However, when different transformation types are mixed into a single robustness
score, the resulting drop is hard to interpret: it may reflect lexical instability, contamination, semantic
drift, increased difficulty, or failure to transfer a proof strategy. Heavy human curation can improve
quality (Li et al., 2024; Huang et al., 2025), but it does not by itself make the perturbation axis
diagnostic.
To address these limitations, we propose a novel diagnostic evaluation method calledGAP
(Generalisation–and–Perturbation) to deeply analyze the robustness of reasoning. We focus on
two requirements that are elementary in mathematics but distinct for LLMs. The first is symbol-role
binding: if a problem is unchanged except that variables and parameters receive unfamiliar, mislead-
ing, or uninformative names, a robust solver should preserve the binding between each identifier and
its latent mathematical role. The second is proof-plan persistence: if the concrete setting and answer
change but the high-level proof plan remains valid, a robust solver should transfer the plan rather
than restart from surface similarity.
In this work, surface transformations are answer-preserving equivalences, whereas kernel transforma-
tions are proof-plan-preserving rewrites: they change the instance and answer while preserving the
abstract sequence of mathematical moves.
GAP constructs paired variants of the same source problem. Surface renames leave the mathematical
statement, answer, and reference proof path unchanged while changing the identifiers along a spectrum
from descriptive to misleading or garbled. Kernel rewrites change the mathematical setting and
terminal answer while preserving the high-level proof plan. Because both families are applied to
the same canonical items, a model’s behavior can be compared within item rather than only across
unrelated benchmark examples. The goal is not only to measure whether accuracy drops, but to make
the source of the drop experimentally separable.
We instantiate GAP on the full William Lowell Putnam Competition archive from 1938 to 2024
(Gleason et al., 1980; Alexanderson et al., 1985; Kedlaya et al., 2002; 2020), yielding PutnamGAP:
1,051 canonical problems and 5,255 aligned transformed instances. This setting is useful for three
reasons. First, Putnam problems are advanced, proof-heavy, and require multi-step reasoning rather
than only computation. Second, the aligned construction lets us compare original, surface, and kernel
behavior on the same mathematical source item. Third, the transformed variants reduce reliance
on static benchmark memorization and allow fresh instances to be generated prospectively. The
surface variants are deterministic renames, while kernel variants are produced by a robust proof-DAG
pipeline.
Across 18 commercial and open-source models, accuracy drops under all transformed families, with
the largest losses under kernel rewrites, and the kernel–surface gap remains large for the strongest
models and on a newer model (Appendix B.3). However, the central finding is not merely that
kernel variants are harder. The two transformation families produce qualitatively different failure
signatures. Surface failures drift from the model’s own original-correct trajectory, suggesting a form
of self-inconsistency under changed identifiers. Kernel failures drift from the canonical proof plan

itself, suggesting failure to preserve or transfer the intended strategy. A prefix-restoration intervention
further separates the regimes: an expert-solution prefix substantially improves recovery on surface
failures but has limited effect on kernel failures. Thus, mathematical robustness is not a scalar
property: two models, or two perturbation families, can exhibit similar aggregate drops while failing
for different reasons and responding differently to intervention.
Our contributions are:
1. We propose GAP, a mechanism-aligned perturbation framework that separates answer-preserving
surface renames from proof-plan-preserving kernel rewrites.
2. We instantiate GAP as PutnamGAP, a 6,306-item aligned corpus built from the full Putnam
archive, with deterministic surface generation and verified kernel generation.
3. We evaluate 18 LLMs and show that kernel rewrites cause substantially larger capability losses
than surface renames, even when conditioning on items solved in canonical form, and that this
gap does not close for the strongest models.
4. We provide trajectory-overlap and prefix-restoration analyses showing that surface and kernel
failures correspond to different observable regimes: repairable self-inconsistency versus plan drift
resistant to the same intervention.
2 RELATEDWORK
Competition-level mathematical reasoning.Benchmarks at Olympiad or research level include
MATH (Hendrycks et al., 2021), OlympiadBench (He et al., 2024), Omni-MATH (Gao et al., 2024),
PutnamBench (Tsoukalas et al., 2024), FrontierMath (Glazer et al., 2024), MathArena (Balunovi´c
et al., 2025), LiveAoPSBench (Mahdavi et al., 2025), and IMO-Bench (Luong et al., 2025), whose
problems are altered by experts to limit memorisation. These establish difficulty ceilings but do
not control the perturbation axis, so they measure capability but not robustness; answer-only scores
can also overstate reasoning, as models near human level on AIME answers scored below25% on
USAMO 2025 proofs (Petrov et al., 2025).
Automated perturbation benchmarks.GSM-Symbolic (Mirzadeh et al., 2024) uses templates to
resample numerical values; GSM-Plus (Li et al., 2024) and MATH-Perturb (Huang et al., 2025) add
human-curated semantic and structural modifications; Putnam-AXIOM (Gulati et al., 2025) applies
programmatic rewrites to a subset of Putnam problems; MaSTer (Hou et al., 2026) runs a multi-round
LLM rewrite–verify loop to stress-test targets on GSM8K and MATH-500; RRB (Golikov et al.,
2026) applies 13 deterministic textual perturbations to AIME problems and finds frontier models
largely resilient; and Math-RoB (Yu et al., 2025) synthesises instruction-level modifications. All
demonstrate that brittleness exists, but because they mix transformation types into a single robustness
score, the resulting drop cannot be attributed to a specific failure mechanism.
Perturbation-axis decomposition for mechanism analysis.A smaller line of work aims to
decomposewhymodels fail. Stolfo et al. (2023) introduce a causal framework that quantifies
robustness of arithmetic reasoning under controlled variable swaps. Fragile Reasoning (Han et al.,
2026) applies meaning-preserving surface perturbations to GSM8K and probes internal activations
to localise failure. Es-sebbani et al. (2026) construct parameterised 2-SAT instances that separate
surface from structural difficulty in the logic domain. MATH-Perturb (Huang et al., 2025) splits
perturbations into “easy” (surface) and “hard” (structural) bins, though the hard variants break
equivalence and therefore confound difficulty with transformation type. Yang et al. (2025) classify
failures under numerical variation as calculation or reasoning errors after the fact; GAP instead fixes
the tested invariance before evaluation (Appendix L).
No prior work fully satisfies even two of these three desiderata: Putnam-AXIOM is automated
at competition level but does not decompose the perturbation axis; MATH-Perturb decomposes
at competition level but its hard variants break equivalence; Fragile Reasoning automates and
decomposes but operates on GSM-level problems. GAP is the first framework to satisfy all three
simultaneously.

3 THEGAP FRAMEWORK
3.1 TWO TRANSFORMATION FAMILIES,ONE DIAGNOSTIC SPLIT
GAP appliesequivalence-preservingtransformations τ to a canonical item (x, y, π)– problem
statement x, reference answer y, and an unreleased expert solution path π that the pipeline uses
internally. A model fθ that consumes τ(x) is graded by the same automatic checker as on x, yielding
a binary label z= grade(f θ(τ(x)), yτ )∈ {0,1}, where yτ tracks any change of answer induced by
τ. We then partitionτinto two disjoint super-families:
•T surf: bijective renames of the problem’s identifiers – the free variables (e.g. r, h) and the fixed
parameters introduced by the statement (e.g. a given surface area S). Mathematical constants
such as π and e are left untouched, and the statement length, equation structure, and reference
solution path are preserved. We instantiate four sub-families that span the lexical-cue spectrum
fromhelpfultono signal(DL, DLC, DLM, GS; defined in Figure 1).
•T kern: rewrites that resample numerical and relationalslotsof the problem while preserving
the high-level proof plan. Both the statement and the answer change; the abstract sequence of
mathematical moves does not. We instantiate one variant per source item, denoted KV.
This split is intentional, not cosmetic. Surface variants stress one specific failure mode – whether the
model preserves a stable binding between unfamiliar names and the latent variable roles they denote –
while keeping the proof plan untouched. Kernel variants stress the complementary mode – whether
the model preserves the high-level plan when the mathematical setting moves – while keeping the
local algebra type unchanged. With both families running on the same item, a drop on T surf but not
T kern implicates representation, the converse implicates planning, and a drop on both points to a
more global brittleness. The mechanism analysis in Section 5 tests exactly this distinction; without
the decomposition the question cannot even be posed.
Evaluation matrix.For a corpus of N canonical items and K=K surf +K kern variants per item,
running fθ on every (xi, τ)pair fills a binary matrix Z∈ {0,1}N×(K+1) . The original column gives
the per-item correctness vector e∈ {0,1}N , and the family-wise aggregates hsurf = maj(Z[:,surf] )
and hkern =Z [:,kern] form the substrate for the paired analyses in Section 5: per-item accuracy drops,

3.2 AUTOMATED GENERATION PIPELINES
The two families are produced by two pipelines that share a quality gate but differ sharply in cost.
Both are end-to-end automatable: GAP does not require the human curation step that, for instance,
GSM-PLUSand MATH-PERTURBuse to refine their candidate variants.
Surface pipeline (deterministic, cheap).Each free-variable or fixed-parameter token in the prob-
lem is sent to a singleO3 call, conditioned on its role and its surrounding context, which returns
a candidate replacement string. A deterministic post-validator rejects names that collide with any
other identifier already present in the problem; the accepted string is then routed to one of the
four family bins (DL, DLC, DLM, GS) by a lightweight tagger. Because the operation is a pure
rename, no theorem-proving capability is required and no judge is needed: every accepted variant is
mathematically equivalent by construction. The full corpus of 1,051 items is processed in roughly
fifteen minutes of wall-clock time on a CPU-only node with exponential back-off retries on transient
API errors.
Kernel pipeline (multi-judge, structured).Kernel rewrites are harder because the statement, the
answer, and most of the algebra change while the proof plan must not. We therefore factor the rewrite
through an intermediateproof DAGso that the plan can be preserved by construction rather than by
post-hoc verification. The five stages are illustrated in Figure 2 and proceed as follows.
1. DAG construction.The official solution π is parsed into a directed acyclic graph whose nodes
are intermediate claims and whose edges are local entailments.
2. Method summarisation.Each node is abstracted from a concrete equation into a content-free
method label(e.g. “square always ≥0 ”; “flatten square”; “move terms”), yielding a plan that a
human could re-execute on different inputs.
3. Replacement generation.A new instance of the leaf math object is sampled subject to a
guard range derived from the problem’s own inequalities (e.g. replacing a+b≥2
√
ab with
a+b+c≥2
√
ab+ac).
4.DAG diffusion.The replacement is propagated through the dependent nodes by re-instantiating
each method label with the new operands, yielding a regenerated proof whose step order matches
the abstracted plan exactly.
5. From answer to question.The terminal node is rendered back into a fully natural-language
problem statement, completing the variant.
A repair-and-verify loop sits on top of stages 3–5. Each candidate is shown to J= 5 independent
O3 judges; the candidate isacceptedonly when two consecutive iterations both produce unanimous
accept verdicts (the “two-in-a-row” rule). On disagreement, the suggested patch is applied and
the candidate re-enters the loop, up to T= 15 iterations. Under an empirical single-judge error
rate ε≤0.04 , a union bound over the T−K+ 1 = 14 length-2 windows gives a soundness bound
δ≤14ε KJ <10 −10 on a flawed variant slipping through (Appendix E.6). The protocol survives
even at ε= 0.08 , where δ <10−8. A 10 % manual audit on accepted variants is applied in addition.
Wall-clock time for the full corpus is under three hours on commodity hardware.
3.3 RELIABILITY THROUGH INFORMATION-ASYMMETRIC VERIFICATION
A natural concern is that the kernel pipeline relies on an LLM both to generate and (through judges)
to verify candidate variants. The key observation is that every LLM call in the pipeline performs
verification given a candidate— checking a presented proof against a known answer — rather than
de novo problem solving. This information asymmetry makes the task strictly easier than the original
problem, so the pipeline’s reliability isarchitecturalrather than dependent on the absolute capability
of any single model. Appendix A develops this argument in full, deriving a soundness bound,
stress-testing it under elevated error rates, and cross-checking with independent grader providers and
human audits.

4 PUTNAMGAP INSTANTIATION
4.1 SOURCES AND CONTAMINATION RESISTANCE
PutnamGAP is built on every problem of the William Lowell Putnam Mathematical Competition
from 1938 to 2024, totalling1,051distinct items after deduplication. The vast majority of pre-2017
problems are sourced from the four MAA Press monographs (Putnam I–IV), which are distributed
only as scanned print volumes; we obtain L ATEX for each item via a three-stage routine (manual
problem segmentation, MathPix-based formula-aware OCR, and custom post-filters that fix the
residual symbol errors), then spot-check every item manually before any variant is generated. The
precise breakdown between scanned-only sources and post-2017 problems released under MAA
licence is given in Appendix H; the practical consequence is that for the bulk of the corpus, faithful
machine-readable copies of either the problem or its solution have not been part of any standard web
crawl, which limits the surface area for verbatim leakage into pre-training.
GAP adds a second, prospective layer of contamination resistance. Even on the post-2017 fraction
where leakage is more plausible, the kernel pipeline can resample arbitrarily many fresh items
aftera model is trained: each of the 1,051 source items admits a bounded but non-trivial number
of equivalence-preserving rewrites, so benchmark–training contamination is a generative pipeline
problem rather than a one-shot data problem. We use this property explicitly in Section 5, where
the mechanism analyses are validated on freshly-generated kernel variants whose statements never
existed at the time the evaluated models were trained.

5 FAILUREANALYSIS
5.1 KERNEL VARIANTS BREAK MORE SOLVABLE ITEMS THAN SURFACE VARIANTS
For each model we define the capability-loss rate L=b/(b+s) , where b counts items the model
solves on the original but misses on the variant and s counts items it solves on both. Unlike the raw
accuracy delta, L conditions on items the model can actually solve, so it is not compressed by a
low baseline. Across all 18 evaluated models, Lkern exceeds the mean of Lsurf over the four surface
families (sign test, p= 7.6×10 −6). The median Lkern is 70%; the median Lsurf is 40%. In raw
accuracy terms the median kernel drop is −10.3 pp versus −2.1 pp for the strongest surface family
(the per-model breakdown behind Figure 3 is tabulated with Wilson CIs in Appendix C.1). The
asymmetry quantifies how much more kernel transformations break, but not why. Section 5.2 moves
to the trajectory level, where the two failure modes dissociate.
5.2 SURFACE AND KERNEL FAILURES DISSOCIATE AT THE TRAJECTORY LEVEL
We compute token-Jaccard Cohen’s d (stable vs brittle) under two reference frames: self-anchor
(the model’s own original-correct trajectory, rename-mapped) and canonical anchor (the dataset’s
expert variant solution). Under self-anchor, all 72 surface cells (18 models × 4 variants) are direction-
positive, with mean d= +0.75 and 69/72 reaching p <0.05 . Switching to canonical anchor
collapses the surface effect to mean d= +0.23 ; for kernel variants, canonical anchor yields mean
d= +0.35 across 17/18 models. The within-cell difference ∆d=d self −d canonical has mean +0.52
(95% CI[+0.47,+0.58], cell-level bootstrap), positive in 71/72 cells.
To separate the overlap signal from trajectory length, we stratify each cell into variant-length tertiles
and recompute d: the shortest tertile retains 72/72 direction-positive cells at mean d= +0.84 ,
ruling out length as the driver. A random-pairing noise floor (median Jaccard 0.22) sits well below
the same-problem stable median (0.48), confirming the metric captures problem-specific reasoning
structure. We label the two signatures self-inconsistency (surface trajectories drift from the model’s
own solution) and strategy drift (kernel trajectories drift from the canonical plan); these are names for
the observable pattern, not claims about internal state.
5.3 EXPERT-SOLUTION PREFIXES RESCUE SURFACE FAILURES BUT NOT KERNEL FAILURES
When a model fails a variant of a problem it solved in original form, does giving it the beginning of a
correct solution help it recover? We re-prompt each such flip case under three conditions: null (variant
problem only), canonical_T2 (prefixed with the dataset’s expert solution truncated at the first equation,
after a leakage filter), and own_T2 (prefixed with the model’s own original-correct trajectory, rename-
mapped and identically truncated). Pooling four models across the four surface families, canonical_T2
raises the rebound rate significantly above null in every family (paired McNemar b=90, c=28,
OR = 3.21,p= 8.9×10 −9); on kernel variants the same intervention does not reach significance.
To isolate the contribution of plan content from prefix presence, we compare canonical_T2 against
own_T2: both supply a partial-solution prefix in variant vocabulary, but only canonical_T2 carries
the expert plan. Canonical_T2 wins the paired contrast (b=75, c=36, p= 2.7×10 −4); own_T2 is
indistinguishable from null (p= 0.08).

6 DISCUSSION
6.1 IMPLICATION
The structural dichotomy identifies two failure modes but not their internal substrates; combining
GAP’s decomposition with model-internal probing of open-weight models (Meng et al., 2022;
Belrose et al., 2023; Turner et al., 2023) could localise each mode to specific layers or heads.
The rescue asymmetry also suggests that inference-time interventions are mode-specific: an
expert-solution prefix recovers many surface failures but few kernel failures, so kernel-side
interventions remain an open problem. Finally, behavioural transfer to MATH (Hendrycks et al.,
2021) and ALG514 (Kushman et al., 2014) (Appendix A.4) shows that the pipeline generalises, but
the trajectory-level dichotomy has so far been established only on PutnamGAP.
6.2 CONCLUSION
Our paper tries to convey two messages. The first is methodological: equivalence-preserving
transformations of mathematical problems can be cleanly decomposed into two disjoint, mechanism-
aligned families (surface renames and kernel rewrites), and the resulting construction is generative,
so the same pipeline can be re-applied after any future model release to produce fresh, contamination-
resistant items and re-audit which components of reasoning are preserved. We realise this construction
asGAP, and PUTNAMGAP, the benchmark we publish, is one instance of GAP applied to the full
Putnam archive; the methodology itself is the contribution, and we expect GAP to remain a useful
instrument for examining mathematical reasoning in models that do not yet exist. The second is
empirical: the dominant remaining weakness in current LLMs’ advanced mathematical reasoning is
not surface fragility but an inability to carry a high-level proof plan through a change of mathematical
setting. Across 18 commercial and open-source models evaluated on PUTNAMGAP, accuracy falls
under every transformed family, but the loss is dominated by kernel rewrites, and the kernel–surface
gap does not close for the strongest models. This, rather than further compression of already-small
surface error, is the natural focal point for the next round of work on mathematical reasoning;
Appendix M turns it into concrete recommendations for evaluation and model development.

6.3 LIMITATIONS
We evaluate models under a single zero-shot pass (no test-time scaling); the J=5 kernel judges
are drawn from a single model family; the structural diagnostic requires visible reasoning chains;
PUTNAMGAP is English-only and competition-style; and generating and evaluating the full corpus
incurs non-trivial API cost. Natural extensions are heterogeneous verifier ensembles that pair LLMs
with symbolic checkers (Lean, SMT), porting the two-pipeline construction to adjacent STEM corpora
with different proof structures, and applying surface and kernel transformations during fine-tuning to
test whether the relevant invariances are learnable rather than incidental.

A PIPELINE ROBUSTNESS ANALYSIS
A.1 ROBUSTNESS OF SURFACE VARIATIONS
Surface variations are produced by deterministic string substitution from the dataset’s rename map. A
post-validator rejects any collision with existing identifiers. Because the operation is a pure rename —
no equation, constant, or solution step is altered — mathematical equivalence holds by construction.
No judge, no grading model, and no theorem-proving capability is involved. The structural-overlap
analysis in §5.2, which is the paper’s headline mechanistic finding, is computed entirely from these
deterministic rename maps and the models’ own trajectories, with zero LLM annotation at any stage.
13

Preprint
A.2 ROBUSTNESS OF KERNEL VARIATIONS
Every LLM call in the kernel pipeline operates in an information-asymmetric verification role: the
judge sees the original problem, the reference solution, the regenerated proof, and the candidate
variant, and is asked whether equivalence holds step by step. This is strictly easier than solving
the problem from scratch — the judge need not search the solution space, only check a presented
chain. Under an empirical single-judge error rate ε≤0.04 , the two-in-a-row unanimous rule across
J=5 judges and T=15 repair iterations yields a soundness bound δ≤14ε KJ <10 −10 on a flawed
variant surviving the loop. The bound holds at ε= 0.08 with δ <10−8. The gate is active in practice:
64 of the 1,051 initial candidates (6.1 %) failed at least one round and entered repair, and all were
eventually accepted (Appendix E.4). A 10 % manual audit on accepted variants found no substantive
errors.
A.3 ROBUSTNESS OF GRADING
Three independent providers (GPT-4o, Claude-Sonnet-4, Gemini-2.5-flash) grade the same 50 strati-
fied solutions; pairwise Cohen’sκ ranges from 0.96 to 1.00, with agreement on 49–50 of 50 cases. On
calculation problems, a strict string-match grader runs in parallel with the LLM grader; disagreement
is below 1 %. A separate 10 %random-sample human audit on the full grading run confirms precision
and recall above97 %.
A.4 ROBUSTNESS ACROSS DATASETS
We apply the same surface pipeline to 159 MATH Level 4–5 problems (Hendrycks et al., 2021) (six
models) and to ALG514 (Kushman et al., 2014) (one model) to verify that the behavioural asymmetry
is not specific to Putnam. All 12 (model, surface-variant) cells on MATH show negative paired drops;
8 of 12 are individually significant by McNemar at p <0.05, with the largest being gpt-4.1-nano
on GS ( −21.4 pp, p= 1.2×10 −6). On ALG514, accuracy falls from 93.6% to 90.9% under DLC
(p= 0.024 ) and to 89.3% under GS (p= 0.002 ; Appendix D.7). We claim behavioural transfer of
the framework, not mechanism-level reproduction: trajectory-level dissociation on these corpora
requires trajectory-capturing reruns and is left to future work.
A.5 THE VERIFICATION GAP AS A DESIGN PRINCIPLE
GAP is designed around a structural asymmetry between the solver and the verifier. The models
under evaluation must solve a problem from scratch with no reference answer; every LLM inside the
pipeline — generators, judges, graders — operates with the reference answer, the canonical solution,
or both already in hand. Checking a presented proof against a known answer is strictly easier than
producing one, so the pipeline does not require frontier-level reasoning from any of its components.
This verification gap is what makes multi-judge consensus, bounded repair loops, and cross-provider
agreement effective: each mechanism amplifies a base reliability that is already high because the task
is verification, not generation.

D.1 PAIRED STRUCTURAL OVERLAP
Canonicalisation protocol.For each surface variant ( DL, DLC, DLM, GS) the dataset stores
a deterministic rename map π:V canonical → Vvariant under variants[v].map. Given a model
trajectory t (variant-named) and the corresponding original-correct trajectory to (canonical-named),
we apply word-boundary substitution that maps each canonical name v∈ Vcanonical to a placeholder
__Vi__ in to, and each variant name π(v) to the same placeholder __Vi__ in t. Both trajectories
then live in a shared placeholder space and are directly comparable. The substitution is applied
longest-name-first to avoid prefix collisions (e.g.aleatingalmondtree).
Drift vs collapse stratification.Pairs are stratified into three groups:stable(original correct,
variant correct),brittle-drift(original correct, variant wrong, variant trajectory ≥200 characters and
at least 25% of the original-trajectory length), andbrittle-collapse(original correct, variant wrong,
variant trajectory shorter than either threshold). Collapse rates are reported separately in §D.4; the
structural-overlap analysis uses only stable and drift pairs.
Metrics.We compute six metrics on the canonicalised pair (to, t)and report Cohen’s d for stable
vs. brittle within each (model, variant) cell, plus Mann–Whitney U and a 1,000-iteration percentile
bootstrap CI on Cohen’s d. The metrics are: (i) token Jaccard; (ii) bigram Jaccard; (iii) directional
coverage |T(t o)∩T(t)|/|T(t o)|; (iv) first-600-token window Jaccard; (v) first-600-token window
bigram Jaccard; (vi) equation-set Jaccard, where math blocks are extracted via regex (display math,
inline $...$, equation/align environments) and trivial fragments shorter than 8 characters are
filtered. Metrics (i) and (ii) are the headline; the remainder serve as robustness checks.
Random-pairing noise floor.For each (model, variant) cell we additionally compute the metric
on N= 400 random pairs (toi , tj) drawn from the stable group with i̸=j . This shuffles the
same-problem signal and provides a baseline that the in-pair Jaccard must exceed for the metric to
be meaningful. Across all 72 surface cells, the random-pairing median Jaccard is approximately
0.18, versus a same-problem stable median in the range 0.36–0.59 and a brittle median in the range
22

Preprint
0.32–0.53. The same-problem signal is therefore roughly twice the noise floor, and the stable–brittle
gap is meaningful within that signal envelope.

cell, with means d= +0.71 and d= +0.69 respectively. The self-anchor signal is therefore not a
length artefact: the shortest tertile, which is the worst case for length confound, carries the strongest
separation.

D.3 QUALITATIVE ARCHETYPES OF CANONICAL-PREFIX RESCUES
A purely quantitative rebound delta does not saywhatcanonical prefixes supply that own prefixes
lack. To approach this we manually audited 12 paired (canonical_T2, own_T2) cases drawn
from the surface arm, stratified across the four rescue models and four variant families. Five recurring
26

Preprint
archetypes emerged; each archetype names what the canonical prefix introduces beyond the model’s
own renamed prefix.
1. Closed-form ansatz.The canonical prefix names the functional family of the answer (e.g. “the
answer is of the forma+b √n”), reframing the task from discovery to verification of constants.
2. Structural commitment.The canonical prefix commits to a global structural choice (e.g.
“parametrise the curve by x=t 2, y=t 3”; “apply Lagrange multipliers”) that the model’s own
original prefix had not yet declared by theT2cut.
3. Ordering of decomposition.The canonical prefix fixes the order in which sub-problems are
addressed (e.g. “handle the symmetric case first; the general case follows by reduction”); the
own-prefix tends to start with the general case and stall.
4. Choice of working basis.The canonical prefix picks an algebraic basis or coordinate system (e.g.
polynomial vs. matrix form; Cartesian vs. polar) that the own-prefix had not yet committed to.
5. Dimensional or sign discipline.The canonical prefix flags an invariant the model needs to
respect (e.g. a sign condition on a discriminant, a dimensional check on a guard inequality); the
own-prefix omits the flag.
In the audited 12 cases, every successful canonical-rescue case fits at least one archetype, and 7 of
the 12 fit two simultaneously (“ansatz + structural commitment” is the most common pair). 55–60%
of surface flips remain unrescued under canonical_T2 even when one of the five archetypes is
present, so the canonical scaffold is necessary but not sufficient.

D.5 INFORMATION-ASYMMETRY DEFENCE FOR THELLMPIPELINE
The structural analysis is zero-LLM by construction.The headline structural-overlap finding
– d= +0.75 self-anchor vs d= +0.23 canonical-anchor across 72/72 surface cells – is computed
entirely without LLM grading or labelling. The rename maps are read directly from the dataset’s
deterministic map field; the metric is token Jaccard on canonicalised text; the stratification into stable
and brittle pairs uses the original solver/grader correctness label that the paper already requires for any
analysis. No additional LLM is invoked. The main mechanistic conclusion of the paper is therefore
independent of LLM grader/generator quality.
Solver vs verifier task asymmetry.Where GAP does invoke LLMs (kernel-variant generation,
kernel-variant verification, and grading), they operate in an information-asymmetric verification role
rather than a solver role.
• Thesolvertask receives only the problem statement Q and must produce the answer A from
scratch.
• Theverifiertask receives Q, the reference answerAref, and a candidate variant or candidate solution;
it must judge equivalence or correctness.
Verification under ground truth is fundamentally easier than from-scratch generation because the
verifier can compare againstA ref and need not search the solution space. An LLM that is not strong
enough to solve a Putnam problem can still reliably check whether two formulations are equivalent
given the solution. This is why our pipeline can use mid-tier LLMs as judges and still produce reliable
variants, and why the capability tier of any single judge is not the limiting factor.
Multi-judge protocol.For kernel-variant generation we use a 5-judge unanimous protocol with
explicit repair loops (Algorithm 1): a candidate variant is accepted only if all five judges agree it is
mathematically equivalent to the original on a battery of probe inputs; otherwise the proposer revises
and the cycle repeats up toT= 15 iterations. Reliability of GAP comes from the protocol architecture
– multi-judge consensus, repair loops, cross-provider agreement, spot audits, and zero-LLM structural
metrics for the surface-side headline – rather than from the capability tier of any single model in the
pipeline.

D.9 PILOT OBSERVATION:SPONTANEOUS VARIANT-TO-CANONICAL NORMALISATION
During development of the rescue pipeline we observed an instructive phenomenon on a subset of
own_T2 cases. When the rename map for an item assigns variant names that overlap with English
vocabulary – for example a parameter a renamed to coefalpha on item 1989-B-1, where the prose
contains the article “a” in “a randomly chosen point” – the mechanical word-boundary substitution
rewrites the article as well, producing prose like “coefalpha randomly chosen point P = (horizcoor,
verticcoor)”. The model often silently restores the article to “a” and replaces the variant variable
names with their canonical originals during continuation, indicating an implicit attempt to repair
semantically broken prose. Across 80 own_T2 trajectories the median variant-name preservation
ratio is 1.0, so this behaviour is the exception rather than the rule, but the existence of the behaviour
is itself diagnostic: when the prefix is internally inconsistent, the model’s recovery move is to
canonicalise, which is consistent with the broader claim that surface failures involve fragile bindings
between renamed names and the latent variable identities.
E VARIANT-GENERATION PIPELINES AND SOUNDNESS BOUND
This appendix gives the long-form description of the surface and kernel pipelines summarised in §3.2,
together with the soundness analysis backing theδ <10 −10 claim used there.
E.1 SURFACE PIPELINE
The surface pipeline applies a bijective rename τ∈ Tsurf to the free-variable and fixed-parameter
tokens of an item. Scientific constants such as π and e are excluded from the rename inventory. A
singleO3 call proposes a candidate replacement conditioned on the token’s role (“free variable” vs.
“fixed parameter”); a deterministic post-validator rejects any name that collides with another identifier
already present in the problem, and the accepted candidate is routed by a lightweight tagger to one of
four family bins. We instantiate one variant per item per family, yielding 1,051×4 = 4,204 surface
items.
The four families span a spectrum of lexical informativeness:
1. Descriptive-Long (DL).A single, semantically helpful English phrase (e.g.
populationDensity). DL is the maximally-informative end of the spectrum.
2. Descriptive-Long-Confusing (DLC).A concatenation of 2–5 unrelated common words (e.g.
walnutVioletTerrace). The composition is grammatical English but supplies no role hint.
3. Descriptive-Long-Misleading (DLM).A phrase built from mathematical jargon that suggests
a different concept than the one the variable plays (e.g. primeFieldOrder used as a real
variable), designed to probe whether models latch onto spurious lexical cues.
4. Garbled-String (GS).A 4–16 character alphanumeric hash (e.g. xcQ7h2ZfRw9v). GS elimi-
nates any linguistic signal.
Because the operation is a pure rename, no theorem-proving capability is required and no judge is
needed. Mathematical equivalence is guaranteed by construction.

E.2 KERNEL PIPELINE
The kernel pipeline implements the five-stage procedure summarised in Figure 2.
1. DAG construction.The official solution π is parsed into a directed acyclic graph whose nodes
are intermediate claims and whose edges are local entailments.
2. Method summarisation.Each node is abstracted from a concrete equation into a content-free
method label(e.g. “square always ≥0 ”; “flatten square”; “move terms”), yielding a plan that a
human could re-execute on different inputs.
3. Replacement generation.A new instance of the leaf math object is sampled within a guard range
derived from the problem’s own inequalities (e.g. replacing a+b≥2
√
ab with a+b+c≥
2
√
ab+ac).
4.DAG diffusion.The replacement is propagated through the dependent nodes by re-instantiating
each method label with the new operands, yielding a regenerated proof whose step order matches
the abstracted plan exactly.
5. From answer to question.The terminal node is rendered back into a fully natural-language
problem statement, completing the variant.
A repair-and-verify loop sits on top of stages 3–5. Algorithm 1 (Appendix F) gives the pseudocode.
Within one iteration t we launch J= 5 independentO3 judges, each returning accept or reject;
an iteration ispassedwhen all J judges return accept. A candidate isacceptedwhen two
consecutive iterations both pass (the “two-in-a-row” rule); on disagreement the suggested patch
is applied and the candidate re-enters the loop, up to T= 15 iterations. A 10% manual audit on
accepted variants follows.
E.3 WHAT A KERNEL REWRITE PRESERVES
The invariant of a kernel rewrite is the high-level proof plan: the dependency structure of the proof
DAG and the method label attached to each node. It isnotline-by-line algebra. Changing the
mathematical setting and the answer necessarily changes local expressions, so a kernel variant whose
intermediate equations matched the source exactly would not be a kernel variant at all.
Figure 1 illustrates the distinction. The source problem (1938-A-2) optimises a buoy built from a
cylinder and two cones; its kernel rewrite uses a single cone of altitude h/2 and a different painted-
area convention. The constraint, the terminal equation, and the answer all change, yet both solutions
execute the same five moves: express surface area and volume in r, h; eliminate one variable using
the constraint; differentiate; check the endpoints; and translate the optimum into the requested
ratio. The machine-readable record in Appendix K stores these moves as core_steps and the
changed parameters as mutable_slots. Because stage 4 regenerates the proof node by node from
the abstracted plan, this correspondence is enforced by construction, and the regex check in §E.7
compares regenerated step lists against the abstracted plans.

the loop’s safety margin is already more stringent than 10−9, which makes an extra symbolic guard
unnecessary.

step list with its abstracted plan found no mismatches.

The raw sources are scanned PDFs; no machine-readable LATEX is provided. The three-stage routine

commonly leaves: spurious \mathrm wrappers, mis-segmented subscripts, and the most frequent

I.4 OTHER OBSERVATIONS
Reasoning-loop time-outs.A few reasoning-trained models occasionally enter token-level loops
during their own thinking trace and exhaust the time budget. In our single-shot evaluation those
instances count as failures, which is the conservative choice for a public benchmark; re-running the
affected items as PASS@k would change the headline numbers slightly upward without affecting the
surface/kernel asymmetry, since the loops are not specifically triggered by transformed variants.

M EXTENDED RECOMMENDATIONS
Forevaluation, aggregate accuracy or a single robustness index over mixed transformation types
conflates the two failure modes. At minimum, surface and kernel drops should be reported separately,
and where feasible paired structural-overlap and prefix-restoration rebound rates should accompany
accuracy, so that readers can distinguish representation-level fragility from plan-level fragility without
re-running the models.
Formodel development, the dichotomy suggests distinct levers for each failure mode. On the surface
side, enforcing self-consistency of reasoning chains — so that a model’s trajectory remains stable
under identifier permutation — would directly target the representation-level fragility documented in
§5.2; training-time consistency regularisation and inference-time self-verification are both plausible
vehicles. On the plan side, the present findings suggest three further directions. First, supervision
that explicitly pairs different surface and kernel forms of the same proof plan, so that plan invariance
becomes a training target rather than an incidental side-effect of large-scale exposure. Second,
inference-time retrieval over a structurally indexed bank of solved instances, so that a candidate
plan is conditioned on plans known to transfer rather than on surface-similar text. Third, decoding
strategies that commit to a high-level plan sketch before instantiating it, exposing plan choice as an
auditable intermediate rather than leaving it implicit in the trajectory. Direct treatment of plan-level
44

Preprint
fragility, rather than another round of surface-level robustification, is the route we believe most likely
to convert further capability gains into durable mathematical reasoning.
